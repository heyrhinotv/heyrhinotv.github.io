Half-life = how long your body takes to clear half the caffeine. For a lot of adults it's around 3–6 hours. Smokers clear it faster (about 3.5 h in one study). Estrogen birth-control pills slow it a lot, and in the last weeks of pregnancy it can stretch to about 10.5 hours.

Where these numbers come from

  1. Typical can ≈ 200 mg, about two cups of brewed coffee: Harvard T.H. Chan, The Nutrition Source
  2. 16 oz energy drinks range 54–328 mg; ~400 mg/day for most healthy adults; experts advise against energy drinks for kids and teens: FDA, “Spilling the Beans”
  3. Half-life ~3–6 h; smokers ~3.5 h; slower on estrogen pills; up to ~10.5 h late in pregnancy; ~99% absorbed within ~45 min: Liu & Xu, Hereditas (2026)
  4. 400 mg six hours before bed cut sleep by more than an hour; avoid caffeine after 5 p.m.: Drake et al., J Clin Sleep Med 2013 (AASM)

Everyone's body is different. This is education, not medical advice. If you're pregnant, have a heart condition, or take medication, ask your doctor about caffeine.
